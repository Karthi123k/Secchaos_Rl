Distributed cloud-native microservices increase operational attack surfaces and create complex dependency failure cascades across services. Although Security Chaos Engineering (SCE) uncovers vulnerabilities through proactive fault injection, current practices remain manual, declarative, and open-loop. Automating runtime remediation introduces an operational dilemma: aggressive mitigations, such as container restarts and network isolation, often cause cascading timeouts and service outages. This paper presents SecChaos-RL, an autonomous closed-loop resilience framework combining SCE with deep reinforcement learning. Formulated as a multi-objective Markov Decision Process, the framework balances security risk reduction, service availability, Mean Time to Recovery (MTTR), and mitigation cost. The neural policy uses Multi-Head Self-Attention over dependency telemetry, Threat-Aware Feature Gating, an Auxiliary Risk Prediction Head, and Threat-Adaptive Temperature Exploration. Across 30 independent random seeds under multi-stage security chaos, empirical results demonstrate critical operational trade-offs: although static alert rules achieve higher local risk reduction (55.5%), they cause severe action churn (30.8% false mitigation rate). SecChaos-RL achieves balanced multi-objective stability, recovering significantly faster under cascading failure than conservative heuristics (MTTR = 1.79 steps versus 2.29 for Threshold-Based, p = 0.0013), halving unnecessary dispatches (20.9% false mitigation rate), and deciding actions within 0.44 ms. The framework establishes practical deployment boundaries between deterministic heuristics and reinforcement learning control.
